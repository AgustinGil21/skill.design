# Workflow: ideas, directions and feedback on ideas

Two situations, one attitude. When the person asks for ideas, give **options worth choosing between**, with a
recommendation. When the person brings an idea, give **honest, useful feedback**, the way a senior designer would in
a critique: specific, grounded in the product's audience and goals, neither flattering nor dismissive.

Read the design context first (`workflows/start.md`); ideas without audience and tone are decoration. Deep guidance:
`knowledge/31-critique-feedback-and-options.md`.

## A. Generating directions

### 1. Pin the brief

Restate the goal, the audience and situation, the constraints and the tone in three lines. If the goal is unclear,
clarify it first: options for an unclear problem are noise (`knowledge/20-problem-framing.md`).

### 2. Diverge on purpose

Produce more ideas than you will show, from different angles:

- **The conventional, done excellently**: what the category does, with craft. Often the right answer.
- **Reframe the problem**: solve the underlying need differently (fewer steps, a different object, no screen at
  all).
- **Borrow from another domain**: how a game, a newspaper, a bank or a physical object handles the same job.
- **Push one quality to the extreme**: radically simple, radically fast, radically personal, radically visual.
- **The contrarian option**: the opposite of what competitors do, if it serves the audience.

Use references: patterns in `knowledge/patterns/`, cases in `knowledge/cases/`, and Mobbin examples when a Mobbin tool
is available.

### 3. Converge to two to four directions

Keep the ones that are **genuinely different** (structure, interaction or concept, not color) and **viable** for
this product. Drop ideas that fail the audience, the stakes or accessibility, even if they look impressive.

### 4. Present them

Use `templates/directions.md`: each direction with its idea in one sentence, what it looks and feels like, why it
fits, risks, effort and a sketch or reference. Then compare them on explicit criteria and **recommend one**, saying
what is given up. A list of options without a recommendation hands the hard part back to the person.

Make directions visible when words are not enough: an ASCII layout sketch in the conversation, or a quick demo
(`workflows/prototype.md`) — offer the demo, build it when the person wants to see.

### 5. Close with a next step

Choose, combine parts, demo one or more, or build. When the person combines parts of different directions, check
that the result still has one idea.

## B. Feedback on the person's idea

Use `templates/idea-feedback.md`.

1. **Restate the idea and the problem it solves.** Misunderstanding the idea makes all feedback wrong. If the problem
   is unclear, ask about the problem before judging the solution.
2. **Separate taste from judgment.** Judge against the audience, the goals, the stakes, principles and evidence, not
   personal preference. When it is taste, say "this is taste".
3. **Start with what genuinely works**, specifically ("putting the fee status next to each member saves the admin a
   trip to the payments screen"), not as a courtesy sandwich.
4. **Name risks with reasons and likelihood**: usability, accessibility, edge cases, states, localization, trust,
   effort, maintenance. Rank them; not every risk matters.
5. **Surface hidden assumptions** and how to test each one cheaply.
6. **Offer alternatives** when they are genuinely better for some situations, not to show off.
7. **Recommend**: go, go with changes, test first, or don't — and what would change your mind.
8. **Offer the next step**: a demo, a test with five people, sketches of variants, building it.

### Tone of feedback

- Direct and kind: "This will confuse coaches because…" rather than "Maybe consider possibly…".
- Specific: point to the element, the user, the moment.
- Proportionate: a small idea gets short feedback.
- Honest when the idea is good: say so plainly and move to execution details. Inventing problems to look rigorous is
  as unhelpful as flattery.
- Honest when the idea is weak: say why, and help find a better path to the same goal.

## Failure modes to avoid

- Options that are the same idea in different colors.
- No recommendation.
- Flattery ("Great idea!") or reflexive criticism.
- Feedback on aesthetics when the problem is structural.
- Ignoring the brand and tone already established.
