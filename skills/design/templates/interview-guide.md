# Interview guide: [topic]

**Research question:** [the decision this research informs, in one line]
**Who:** [participant profile and how many; 5–8 per distinct segment is a common starting point for interviews]
**Length:** [30–45 minutes] · **Format:** [remote / in person] · **Recording:** [only with consent]

Rules for the interviewer: ask about **past behavior**, not opinions or predictions ("Tell me about the last time…",
not "Would you use…?"). Open questions, one at a time. Follow the story with "What happened next?", "Why?", "How did
you decide?". Do not show or pitch the design. Silence is fine. Write down exact words for key moments.

## Opening (3 minutes)

- Thanks, purpose in one sentence ("We're learning how people [activity], not testing you"), how long it takes.
- Consent to record, how the recording is used and stored, that they can stop at any time.

## Context (5–10 minutes)

- Tell me about your role / your week / how [activity] fits into it.
- What tools or people do you rely on for [activity] today?

## The last time (15–20 minutes)

- Tell me about the last time you [did the activity]. Walk me through it from the start.
- What were you trying to get done? What triggered it?
- Where did it get hard, slow or annoying? What did you do then?
- What did you do instead, or how did you work around it?
- How did you know it was done? What happened afterwards?
- Is that typical? When does it go differently?

## Priorities and stakes (5 minutes)

- What would happen if [it went wrong / was late]? Who else is affected?
- If you could change one thing about how this works today, what would it be?

## Closing (2 minutes)

- Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
- Can we contact you again? Thanks; incentive details.
