# Slop catalog: copy

Interface and marketing copy in any language. Read with the [catalog rules](README.md): every item is banned by default,
and keeping one needs a stated reason tied to evidence.

Background: [interface writing](../04-interface-writing.md) · [content and localization](../24-content-and-localization.md)
· [persuasive writing](../landing/04-persuasive-writing.md).

Tells are stated by function and illustrated in English. Every language has its own stock phrases with the same
function; the ones listed for Spanish, Portuguese, French and German below are examples, not the full list. When
writing in another language, look for the equivalents.

## Headlines

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Supercharge / Unlock / Elevate / Transform / Revolutionize your X" | Hype verb plus a noun; could describe any product | Say what the person gets, in their words: "Close the month in two days instead of ten" |
| "The future of X" / "X, reimagined" / "X for the modern era" | Says nothing about what is new | Name the change concretely |
| "The all-in-one platform for X" | Category word plus filler | Name what it combines and why that matters |
| "Built for X teams" / "Designed for builders" | Audience flattery without a claim | Name the specific job that this audience does with it |
| "Where X meets Y" | Rhetorical template | One plain sentence about what it does |
| "Your X, your way" / "X made simple" / "X, simplified" | Empty promise of ease | Show what is simpler: fewer steps, a specific removed chore |
| "Meet X" + product name as the H1 | Introduces without informing | Lead with what it does; the name is in the logo |
| "Stop X. Start Y." | Overused antithesis | One sentence with the concrete before and after |
| Question headlines ("Tired of spreadsheets?") | Sales-page reflex; the answer is always yes | State the situation and the solution |
| Headline that is a pun or wordplay | Does not translate, delays understanding | Clear first; wit only if the brand voice has it and it survives translation |
| H1 that only works with the subhead | The headline is decorative | The H1 alone says what the product does or what the page is for |

## Subheads, body and feature copy

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adjective triads: "Fast, simple and secure", "Create. Collaborate. Grow." | Rhythm with no facts | One verifiable fact per claim: "Pages load in under a second on a 4G connection" |
| Filler adjectives: seamless, effortless, powerful, intuitive, robust, cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class, best-in-class, state-of-the-art | Unverifiable; every product says them | Delete the adjective and add the evidence |
| "It's not just X, it's Y" / "More than just X" | The most recognizable generated construction | Say what it is |
| "Whether you're a X or a Y, …" | Generic audience sweep | Pick the audience the page is for |
| "In today's fast-paced world…" / "In an ever-evolving landscape…" | AI preamble | Start with the point |
| Words like delve, tapestry, testament, realm, embark, navigate (the landscape), harness, foster, bolster, beacon, pivotal, robust, holistic, synergy, paradigm | Generated-text vocabulary | Plain verbs and nouns |
| Feature names as benefits: "Smart Insights", "AI-Powered Analytics", "Advanced Reporting" | Labels, not outcomes | What the person learns or does: "See which invoices will be paid late" |
| "Lightning-fast", "blazing fast", "rock-solid" | Clichés replacing numbers | The real number, measured under stated conditions |
| "Join thousands of happy customers" | Invented social proof | A real, attributable number, or none |
| "No credit card required" repeated under every button | Fine once; repeated, it becomes noise | Say it once next to the first sign-up action |
| Every sentence the same length and structure | Generated cadence | Vary rhythm; short sentences for facts, longer ones to explain |
| Overuse of em dashes, colons and "Here's the thing:" | Generated punctuation tics | Simple sentences; use punctuation for meaning |
| Colon headlines everywhere: "Analytics: Reimagined" | Template pattern | A sentence |
| Exclamation marks in interface text | Forced enthusiasm | Calm, specific statements |
| "We" talking about ourselves more than "you" doing something | Company-centered copy | The person's task and outcome |

## Buttons and actions

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Get started" as the only CTA everywhere | Says nothing about what happens | Name the next step: "Create your first invoice", "Book a 20-minute demo" |
| "Learn more" links on every card | No information scent | Say what the person will learn: "See how reminders work" |
| "Submit", "OK", "Continue" on actions that charge, delete or commit | Hides the consequence | Name the outcome: "Pay €48", "Delete 12 files", "Send to 340 people" |
| "Let's go!", "Let's do this!", "Yay!" | Forced enthusiasm | The action's name |
| "Click here" | Meaningless out of context and for screen readers | Link the words that describe the destination |
| Two CTAs with the same weight ("Get started" and "Book a demo" both filled) | No primary path | One primary; the other secondary or a link |
| "Try for free" when a card is required and charged after the trial | Misleading | "Start 14-day trial · €12/month after 1 June" |
| Cancel, decline and close buttons with guilt ("No, I don't want to save money") | Confirmshaming | Neutral: "No thanks", "Not now", "Cancel subscription" |

## Microcopy, errors and states

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Oops! Something went wrong." | Cute and empty | What happened, whether data is safe, what to do: "We couldn't save the draft. Your text is still here. Try again." |
| "An error occurred. Please try again later." | No cause, no time, no alternative | Cause if known, what was kept, a retry or another path |
| "No data available" / "Nothing here yet" | States the obvious | What will appear here and how to make it appear, with the action |
| "Success!" / "Done!" | No confirmation of what happened | "Invoice sent to accounts@hartley.co" |
| "Are you sure?" | Generic; people click through | Name the action and its consequence: "Delete the 'Q3 Plan' board and its 48 cards? This can't be undone." |
| "Please" and "kindly" on every instruction | Filler politeness | Direct, respectful instructions; "please" when asking for effort |
| "Hey there! 👋" greetings | Generated friendliness | Greet with something useful, or not at all |
| "Welcome back, [Name]!" as the dashboard headline | Fills the most valuable spot | What needs the person's attention today |
| Tooltips that repeat the label | Noise | A tooltip only when it adds information |
| Placeholders as labels ("Enter your email") | Disappear when typing; fail accessibility | A visible label; placeholder for an example format only |
| "Loading…" with no context during long work | People do not know what is happening or for how long | Say what is being done and, when known, how long or how much |
| Notification copy that teases ("You won't believe what happened…") | Engagement bait | The fact: "Ana commented on 'Budget 2027'" |

## Proof, numbers and claims

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Round invented numbers: "10,000+ teams", "99.9% uptime", "10x faster", "50% more productive" | People recognize filler numbers; legal risk | Real numbers with source and date, or a `[data: …]` placeholder |
| Testimonials from "Sarah K., Product Manager at TechCorp" | Fake by construction | Real quotes with permission and full attribution, or no testimonials |
| "Rated 4.9/5" without source | Unverifiable | The source, the count and the date |
| "Trusted by leading companies" over a logo strip | Implies endorsements | Only customers who agreed, named |
| "#1 X tool" / "The best X" | Superlative without evidence | A specific, attributable fact |
| Before/after numbers with no baseline | Persuasion without meaning | Baseline, period, sample and source |

## AI-feature copy

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "AI-powered", "Powered by AI", "with the magic of AI" | The technology is not the benefit | What it does and its limits: "Drafts a reply from the thread. Review before sending." |
| "Ask me anything!" in an assistant with narrow skills | Promise it cannot keep | Say what it can do with examples of real tasks |
| Assistant that says "Great question!" / "I'd be happy to help!" | Filler before the answer | Answer first |
| "Our AI understands you" / human-like claims | Anthropomorphism and overclaiming | Describe behavior; state uncertainty and sources |
| "Generating magic… ✨" while loading | Cute and uninformative | What is being done ("Reading 14 files") |

## Equivalents in other languages (examples)

| Language | Common stock phrases with the same function |
| --- | --- |
| Spanish | "Lleva tu X al siguiente nivel", "potencia", "revoluciona", "de forma sencilla", "sin complicaciones", "todo en un solo lugar", "la solución definitiva", "descubre", "impulsa tu negocio", "en la era digital", "no es solo X, es Y", "diseñado para ti" |
| Portuguese | "Leve seu X ao próximo nível", "potencialize", "revolucione", "de forma simples", "tudo em um só lugar", "a solução completa", "descubra", "impulsione" |
| French | "Propulsez", "boostez", "révolutionnez", "en toute simplicité", "tout-en-un", "la solution ultime", "découvrez", "à l'ère du numérique" |
| German | "Bringen Sie Ihr X auf das nächste Level", "revolutionieren", "nahtlos", "mühelos", "All-in-One-Lösung", "die Zukunft von", "entdecken Sie" |

Also check: mixed forms of address (tú and usted, du and Sie) in one product; anglicisms the audience would not use;
English word order translated literally ("Empieza ahora gratis hoy").

## Before calling copy done

- [ ] Every headline says what the product does or what the page is for, without the subhead.
- [ ] No hype verb, filler adjective, triad or "not just X" construction remains, in any language of the product.
- [ ] Every button names its outcome; consequences (money, deletion, sending) are explicit.
- [ ] Every number, quote and logo is real and attributable or marked `[data: …]`.
- [ ] Errors say what happened, what was kept and what to do.
- [ ] The voice is the product's, consistent in form of address, in the product's language.
