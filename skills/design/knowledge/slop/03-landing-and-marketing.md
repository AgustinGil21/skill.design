# Slop catalog: landing pages and marketing

The sections and structures that make a landing page look assembled from a template. Read with the
[catalog rules](README.md): every item is banned by default, and keeping one needs a stated reason tied to evidence.

Background: [landing pages](../landing/README.md) · [strategy and structure](../landing/01-strategy-and-structure.md) ·
[hero](../landing/02-hero.md) · [sections](../landing/03-sections.md).

## Page structure

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The canonical stack: nav, hero, logo strip, three features, bento, testimonials, pricing, FAQ, CTA band, footer | The page is a template filled in, not an argument | Start from the visitor's questions and objections in order; each section answers one; drop sections with nothing true to say |
| Every section the same height and pattern | No pacing; the eye has nowhere to rest or speed up | Vary density and form: a sentence, a demo, a table, a quote, a single image |
| Sections that exist because "landing pages have them" (FAQ with five invented questions, a "How it works" with three steps of nothing) | Filler | Include a section only with real content; real FAQs come from sales and support |
| The same CTA pair repeated after every section | Pressure without new reasons | One primary action, repeated where a new reason to act has been given |
| A page that never shows the actual product | Hides what is sold | Real screens, a real demo, the real object, early |
| Long page for a simple, known product | Scroll for its own sake | As long as the decision needs |

## Hero

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pill badge above the headline ("✨ New: AI features", "Backed by Y Combinator", "v2.0 is here →") | The most recognizable template element | Put real news where it matters; if there is a launch, say what launched in the headline or a dated note |
| Centered headline, grey subhead, two buttons (filled + outline), nothing else | Template default | Compose from the message: product shown, a real input to try, a specific proof point |
| Headline with gradient text or one highlighted word in a gradient | Signature of generated pages | Solid text; emphasis by weight or a meaningful word in the accent |
| Product screenshot in a tilted 3D perspective with a glow behind | Decoration that makes the product unreadable | Flat, readable screen at a size where people can see the details, or an interactive demo |
| Floating UI fragments (notification cards, avatars, chips) orbiting the hero | Fake activity | Real, legible content of one moment of use |
| Animated gradient mesh or aurora background | Mood instead of message; costly on phones | Calm background; spend the visual budget on the product |
| "Watch demo" button with a play icon that opens a generic video | Promise of a video that rarely helps | A short, captioned demo of the main task, or an interactive one |
| Email field plus "Get early access" for a product that exists | Wait-list template on a live product | The real next step (sign up, try, buy) |
| Social proof line under buttons ("Join 25,000+ users ★★★★★") | Invented or unattributed | A real, sourced proof point, or nothing |

## Proof and trust

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logo strip of grey wordmarks with "Trusted by industry leaders" | Often invented or unlicensed | Real customers with permission; name the segment ("Used by 40 clinics in Andalusia") |
| Infinite marquee of logos | Movement to fake abundance | A static set of real logos, or a named case |
| Testimonial carousel with stock or generated avatars | Fake, and carousels hide content | Two or three real, attributed quotes visible at once, with role and organization |
| Tweet wall of praise screenshots | Easy to fabricate, hard to read | Selected real quotes with links to the source |
| "As featured in" with media logos | Often exaggerated | Link to the actual article |
| Stats strip: "10x", "99.9%", "24/7", "150+ countries" | Filler numbers | Real metrics with source and period, or remove the strip |
| Security badges (SOC 2, GDPR, ISO) without a link to the evidence | Badge decoration | Only real certifications, linked to the trust page |

## Features

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three or six cards with icon, two-word title, one vague line | The default feature grid | Show each important capability with a real example: a screen, a before/after, a real input and output |
| Bento grid where each tile holds a tiny abstract UI mock | Decorative assembly | Fewer, larger, real views of the product doing the thing |
| Feature list with checkmarks and no explanation | No reason to care | Group by the job; explain the one that changes the decision |
| Tabs that switch between generic screenshots | Hides content behind clicks for no gain | Sequence the important ones vertically |
| "How it works" in three numbered steps (Sign up, Configure, Enjoy) | Says nothing about how it works | Show the real steps with real content, or show the result |
| Comparison table against competitors with all checkmarks on your side | Unbelievable | Honest comparison with sources and dates, including where competitors are better, or no table |
| Integration section as a grid of famous logos | Implies integrations that may not exist | Only real integrations, with what each one does |

## Pricing

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three tiers with the middle one highlighted "Most popular" | Popularity claim with no data; template | Highlight from real data, or recommend by audience ("For clinics with more than 3 rooms") |
| Prices like $9 / $29 / $99 invented by the generator | Fake pricing | Real prices or `[data: price]` |
| Feature lists where higher tiers repeat everything plus "Everything in Pro, plus:" with vague items | Hard to compare | A comparison table with the differences that matter |
| "Contact us" for the top tier with no indication of price or what triggers it | Hides the cost | Say who needs it and what changes; a starting price if possible |
| Annual/monthly toggle that shows the monthly-equivalent of annual in big type | Misleading anchor | Show the amount charged and when |

## FAQ, final CTA and footer

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FAQ with questions nobody asks ("Is it easy to use?") | Filler that pretends to answer objections | Real questions from support and sales, with specific answers |
| Final band "Ready to get started?" with the hero's buttons on a gradient | Template close | A final reason and the one next step, or end the page with the last useful content |
| Mega footer with columns of links to pages that do not exist | Scaffolding | Only real pages; contact, legal and language selector |
| Newsletter box "Stay in the loop" with no promise | Collects emails without saying what for | Say what arrives and how often, or remove it |
| "Made with ❤️ in San Francisco" | Cliché | Real address and company details where they matter (legal, trust) |

## Blog, portfolio and editorial

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blog grid of cards with generated cover images | Every post looks the same and none is memorable | Covers only when there is a real image; otherwise a strong typographic list |
| Article titles like "The Ultimate Guide to X in 2026" | SEO slop | Specific titles that say what the reader will learn |
| Portfolio with mock-ups on floating devices and no explanation | Shows surfaces, not thinking | The problem, the decisions, the result, with real artefacts |
| Author bios written in third person with superlatives | Generated bio | A plain, specific bio |
