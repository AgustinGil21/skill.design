# Landing pages for AI tools, physical products and studios

Three categories where the usual SaaS template fails. For AI tools, the output is the argument and trust is the
barrier. For physical products, desire and detail decide, and logistics close the sale. For studios and portfolios,
the work and the experience are the product.

## AI tools (image, video, text generation, agents)

### What visitors need

- **What it produces**, at real quality.
- **How much control they have** over the result.
- **What happens to their data** and inputs.
- **What it costs** per use, and the limits.
- **Whether they may use the results** commercially.

### Patterns

- **The output is the argument**: galleries of generated results grouped by use case (Product animation · Characters ·
  Landscapes) with tabs ([Runway](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/3046f4e0-5ebf-431a-9e83-8f38af865cf2)).
- **The prompt next to the result**, copyable ("Prompt: Image + handheld cinematic shot"), to teach use and prove it
  is real (Runway; [Grok](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/5af303d6-a8a5-4269-ac24-71237c5b01f4)).
- **Input → output**: one uploaded object and all the variants it produces ("One product photo → every shot and
  setting"; [Fourmula AI](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/fb0c8546-6ff0-4c32-95f1-c12085f332b7)).
- **The prompt field in the hero** with use cases (Advertising · E-commerce · Film) and the result beside it
  ([Melius](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/2342f794-d5de-4da0-91cb-110288c39895)).
- **By audience**, with the type of piece each one needs (agencies: concept boards, campaign variants;
  [Melius](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/88144de0-5a78-4ac5-96e5-705daeb6cec2)).
- **AI inside the product they already use**, shown in context (generating a diagram from the canvas;
  [Miro](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/02370808-d73e-4668-be70-22ff0e1ff800)).
- **Control and limits stated**: what can be adjusted, commercial use rights, what happens to data, cost per
  generation.
- **Performance**: galleries with light thumbnails and video on demand; a hero that loads black while an animation runs
  is a failure ([Leonardo AI](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/c4686ac8-4fe3-4040-b442-2b405f65ae33)).

### Honesty

- Results unretouched, or marked if they were edited.
- Cherry-picked examples labeled as best results, with typical results shown too; "results vary" stated plainly.
- Limitations named: what it does badly, languages it handles poorly, kinds of input that fail.
- No anthropomorphizing claims ("understands you", "thinks like an expert") that set wrong expectations.

### Trying it on the page

A live try can make capability tangible when the result is quick and meaningful. It needs rate limits and abuse
protection, truthful queueing and failure states, and a deliberate account boundary. Asking for an account after
a useful sample may reduce friction; upfront authentication can be justified by cost, privacy or action scope. See
[AI first run and sharing](../patterns/ai-first-run-and-sharing.md).

### Accessibility

Generated galleries need alternatives appropriate to their purpose. Text descriptions can explain output details;
video requires the applicable captions and audio description, not an assumption that either replaces the other.
Keep prompts as selectable text where they are part of the evidence.

## Physical products

### What visitors need

What it is and how it looks in detail, how it feels and is used, whether it fits (size, compatibility), the price, the
delivery date, and what happens if it is not right (returns, warranty).

### Patterns

- **The object as protagonist**, annotated with what matters over the photo or 3D (stainless steel · 270 g · 70 mm ·
  no subscription) and the price in the button ("I want mine — €60";
  [Norma](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/1ee75ebd-f4c5-4fdb-99b8-8b3a7bc77175)).
- **In use, in context**: a real person using it where it makes sense ("A computer you can use outside";
  [Daylight](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/a934cc95-697d-4051-8d07-16e8d1a75e34)).
- **Three benefits with icons over the image** of the product in use
  ([Daylight](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/b9729ea8-5d0f-4cd1-bd67-77d6f9d835e0)).
- **Buy without leaving**: size, color, "Add — €189" and shipping stated ("Order within 8 h 20 min and it ships
  today"; [Rains](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/9081af72-4e71-48ea-8398-0adba7e44b4f)); compatibility and
  specifications in a row ([Rivian](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/2fbfce9c-370a-4f9f-b408-64da62aeefdd)).
- **Product details as a list with icons** (format, paper, pages, hanger) and why to buy a refill
  ([Kalstore](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/0ac05517-9aa4-4f82-8e96-f2cce86a6f12)).
- **Real lead times** for made-to-order products ("Production: 12 weeks · Estimated shipping: Feb 9"), samples and a
  downloadable spec sheet ([In Common With](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/ed0fc4a0-b230-47cd-9e75-2c53223b481c)).
- **Shipping, returns and warranty near the button**; reviews with photos.
- See also [commerce and maps](../patterns/commerce-and-maps.md) and [product pages](../patterns/product-pages.md).

### Photography decisions

| Shot | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Clean studio shot | Shape, color, detail; comparison |
| In use | Scale, context, desire |
| Detail close-ups | Material, quality, craft |
| Size reference | Fit and expectations |
| Every color option | Avoid disappointment and returns |

Use accurate color-managed photography and explain material variation where it matters. Displays and lighting
change appearance; offer samples when the purchase risk justifies them rather than promising perfect color matching.

### Markets

Prices in the local currency with tax as expected, shipping countries stated up front, local return rules, sizes in
local systems, and plug or voltage compatibility for electronics.

## Studios, agencies and portfolios

### What visitors need

The quality and kind of work, whether the studio fits their project (discipline, scale, budget), how the studio
thinks, and how to start a conversation.

### Patterns

- **Work first**: selected projects with a strong image, name and client or type
  ([Vucko](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/ceab4ab4-d046-4356-bde4-32acd9781fa8),
  [Unseen Studio](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/79d328bc-800b-4830-9de1-7ef8fd2dd00f)).
- **Discipline filters with counts** (Branding 4 · Digital 17 · Motion 4) and Grid / List views
  ([Unseen Studio](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/79d328bc-800b-4830-9de1-7ef8fd2dd00f),
  [MOUTHWASH](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/9c2b6408-fce2-44db-9c0b-3def5bddba12)).
- **Project facts on the card** (result, year, type; "94 runs · Agents";
  [Cloudstudio](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/48890788-7d16-4887-baaa-5b22583ef055)).
- **Character details**: the studio's local time ("Toronto 4:50"), optional sound off by default, technical labels.
  They add personality without costing clarity.
- **The site can be the demonstration**, but with a way out: an accessible project list, clear navigation, visible
  contact. **Anti-example**: a black loading screen with two words and nothing to do
  ([Phantom Studios](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/5efe2d57-4b21-41cb-9081-e8c3dc0d4ac3)).
- **Low-friction contact**: "Book a call" in view, a copyable email, what kinds of projects they take and typical
  budgets or timelines if possible.
- **Each case with process, decisions and results**, not only beautiful images.

### Case studies for portfolios

A strong portfolio case shows: the brief and constraints, the designer's role (what they did vs. the team), the key
decisions and the alternatives rejected, the result (with numbers where possible and permitted), and what they would
do differently. Confidential work may be shown as anonymized process only when permission and confidentiality terms
allow it.
Removing a client name does not necessarily remove identifying details or publication restrictions.

### Experimental sites

Experimental navigation, WebGL and sound are part of a studio's argument. Keep: a non-experimental path (a plain list
or "skip intro"), reduced-motion behavior, keyboard access to projects, and reasonable load times. Award-winning
experiences that hide contact lose clients.

## What all three categories have in common

The visitor is evaluating something whose quality is hard to infer from a feature list: a generated result, an
object they cannot touch, or judgment they cannot buy from a catalog. The page needs a demonstration and a clear
boundary around what that demonstration proves.

A beautiful output does not establish reproducibility. A product photograph does not establish fit or delivery.
A studio's finished case does not establish that it performed every discipline involved. Senior design makes
these gaps explicit and supplies the evidence appropriate to the actual purchase.

## Questions before choosing the demonstration

### AI tools

- Which job is the visitor evaluating: exploration, a publishable asset, a reliable transformation or delegated action?
- Is the example representative of ordinary inputs, and what selection or editing occurred before publication?
- What can the visitor control, repeat, undo or export? Which limits materially affect usable results?
- Does trying the product upload private material or trigger external actions, and when is that explained?
- What counts as a billable attempt, including failure, cancellation or a rejected result?
- Which supported languages and input types have been evaluated, and where is quality uncertain?

### Physical products

- What failure would be expensive: wrong dimensions, incompatible equipment, unsuitable material or late delivery?
- Which details can photography establish, and which require specifications, samples or a fit guide?
- Are shipping dates estimates or commitments, and do they depend on variant, destination or production status?
- What is included, what must be bought separately, and what ongoing consumables or services are required?
- How does a buyer resolve uncertainty before paying, and what happens if the item still does not fit?

### Studios and portfolios

- Who is deciding: a client, recruiter, collaborator or procurement reviewer?
- Which projects demonstrate the kind of work the studio wants next, rather than only its most famous clients?
- What was the individual's or studio's role, and which collaborators need credit?
- Is the site itself a relevant sample of the offered discipline, or does experimentation obstruct evaluation?
- What constraints, availability and engagement model help a prospect decide whether contact is worthwhile?

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Situation | Option | Why | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slow AI result | Recorded example with inputs | Explains value without queueing | Less direct evidence |
| AI result is quick and bounded | Live sample with limits | Demonstrates control | Cost, abuse and failure handling |
| Physical fit dominates | Dimensions and contextual photography | Reduces uncertainty directly | Less spectacle |
| Shape needs inspection | Optional model and specifications | Reveals relationships | Asset and access complexity |
| Studio sells visual craft | Selected work with role summaries | Quickly shows quality | Deeper cases needed |
| Studio sells process | Decisions and outcomes in cases | Shows reasoning | More reading and editing |
| Confidential work | Approved summary and private review | Protects confidentiality | Less public proof |

Do not add a live AI field if most visitors will receive a queue, an unrepresentative result or an unexplained
signup wall. A transparent recorded example may produce more accurate expectations. If a live trial is needed,
make its restrictions clear before the visitor invests in a detailed prompt or uploads a valuable file.

Do not add a 3D viewer just because a physical product exists. Accurate dimensions and an in-use photograph may
answer the fit objection more directly. A viewer earns its cost when manipulation reveals something that static
views cannot communicate well, and it still needs a usable alternative.

Do not make playful navigation the only way to assess a studio. Experimental work can be a strong sample for a
motion practice, while a hiring manager still needs a direct project list and explicit role attribution.

## States that affect trust

| State | AI tool | Physical product | Studio or portfolio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Loading | Keep prompt and explain queue | Reserve media space | Show project identity before video |
| Partial result | Identify unfinished output | Show missing variant imagery | Explain available case material |
| Unavailable | Name capability restriction | State variant or regional limit | State current engagement availability |
| Failed action | Keep input; clarify attempt cost | Preserve selection | Preserve inquiry content |
| Uncertain receipt | Check generation/request status | Avoid duplicate orders | Avoid claiming the inquiry arrived |
| Permission boundary | Explain input/action access | Request necessary destination data | Explain private-case access |
| Changed condition | Disclose model or limit changes | Reconfirm price and stock | Update roles and availability |
| Success | Separate preview from usable export | Confirm what was ordered | Confirm receipt, not acceptance of work |

The last distinction matters: a result may be generated but not suitable for publication; a cart addition is not
an order; an inquiry received is not a booked engagement. Celebratory copy should follow the actual state.

For a failed AI attempt, distinguish technical failure from an output the visitor dislikes. The refund, retry
and credit policy may differ, so explain it before encouraging repeated generation. Do not imply a guaranteed
result if quality depends on input and selection.

For made-to-order products, a variant change can affect both price and delivery. Keep those dependencies visible
and preserve selection when moving between imagery, dimensions and shipping details. Unavailable variants should
remain understandable where that helps comparison, with availability status rather than an unexplained disabled swatch.

## Evidence and selection bias

A curated gallery is legitimate art direction when its selection is clear. Explain whether results are typical,
selected highlights, edited composites or demonstrations of a feature that is not generally available.
The same input and settings do not necessarily guarantee the same generated output; state reproducibility limits.

For products, use retouching to describe the object accurately rather than conceal seams, scale or texture that
buyers need to evaluate. If photographs show optional accessories, identify them. Lifestyle imagery can create
desire while specification imagery resolves uncertainty; neither needs to pretend to serve both jobs.

For portfolio cases, distinguish delivered work, concept exploration and self-initiated work. Credit team roles
and label unavailable outcome data rather than inventing success. A clear account of constraints and decisions
can demonstrate judgment without a revenue metric that the studio never received permission to publish.

## Accessibility requirements and recommendations

Meaningful images need text alternatives under WCAG 1.1.1. Describing the visual concept may be sufficient for
an artistic sample; a product diagram may need dimensions in text, and a before/after image needs its comparison
explained. Context determines the alternative, not whether the image was generated, photographed or rendered.

Keyboard access is required under 2.1.1, subject to its exception. Drag-based comparison sliders and 3D controls
need suitable non-drag interaction where 2.5.7 applies. A pair of labeled static views is often a useful additional
path, but assess whether it communicates the information needed for the task.

Prerecorded synchronized media needs captions under 1.2.2, with its exception; visual information in video may
need audio description under 1.2.5. A silent decorative loop can often be removed without losing meaning.
Automatic motion beside other content needs pause, stop or hide under the conditions of 2.2.2.

Status messages such as a settled queue update or submission confirmation need exposure under 4.1.3. Announce
meaningful changes, not every progress tick. Visible prompt and form labels help all visitors; custom controls
need name, role and value under 4.1.2.

As recommendations, provide a stable poster, respect reduced motion, keep sound optional and offer a plain project
list. These choices make rich demonstrations easier to use; they do not individually establish full conformance.

## Copy and market-specific meaning

AI copy should distinguish generation, review and execution. In English examples, "Generate a draft" and "Publish"
name different consequences. Preserve the distinction in languages whose shortest natural button labels differ.
Do not imply human understanding merely because a conversational style suits the brand.

Rights and data-use summaries need approved scope, including applicable product tier and policy date where
relevant. A designer should not extrapolate commercial permissions from an example gallery or a model's name.
If policy differs across markets, connect the visitor to the applicable terms before a consequential upload.

Physical-product copy names the unit, variant, included items and delivery basis. Localize dimensions and size
systems while preserving exact source measurements. A converted value should not imply a more precise measurement
than exists. Check decimal separators, voltage, plug types and compatibility in the actual destination market.

Portfolio copy names role, discipline, collaboration and outcome. Titles and professional credentials may carry
different meanings across markets; describe the contribution rather than relying on a prestigious-sounding title.
Contact copy should state what information helps assess fit and what response expectation is genuinely supported.

## Test protocol and measurement

For AI, ask visitors to judge an ordinary input, identify the controls and explain what happens to their data.
Include an unsuitable input and a failed attempt. Measure whether people obtain a usable result with understood
cost, not only whether they submit a prompt. Track retries, abandoned queues and misunderstood rights as guardrails.

For physical goods, ask visitors to select a compatible variant and explain total commitment, delivery and return
conditions. Test comparisons with long localized specifications. Track appropriate completed orders alongside
returns for mismatch, delivery misunderstandings and support requests about information already on the page.

For studios, give reviewers a realistic brief and ask them to find relevant work, describe the studio's role and
start an inquiry. Measure qualified conversations, with wrong-discipline inquiries and inaccessible project access
as guardrails. Time spent in a visual experiment is ambiguous: it may indicate delight or difficulty finding contact.

In all three categories, record what would change the recommendation. Better generation reliability might justify
a live trial; repeated size uncertainty might justify samples; repeated requests for strategy work might change
which portfolio cases lead. Let observed decision problems drive the next addition.

## Worked comparison (fictional design scenario)

**Brief:** an AI tool that generates product photos for small online shops.

**Weak:** "Unleash the power of AI for your brand" over a purple gradient; a gallery of perfect images without prompts
or inputs; no prices until sign-up; nothing about commercial rights or what happens to uploaded photos.

**Strong:** "Create product-photo scenes from a photo of your item." The same fictional tool shows a phone photo
of a ceramic mug beside generated scenes, with input, settings and selection explained. Typical outputs include
visible imperfections. Pricing identifies the billable attempt; approved summaries explain input handling and
usage permissions. A sample trial states its upload and account conditions before the visitor starts.

The stronger treatment narrows the promise to demonstrated capability rather than claiming studio quality for
every input. The mug remains the same across the comparison, so the visitor can judge object fidelity rather than
be distracted by unrelated beautiful images. If preserving labels or shape is unreliable, show that limit too.

A live upload is the recommendation only if latency, input handling and failure recovery support a fair trial.
Otherwise use a recorded demonstration with disclosed conditions and a clear route to a fuller evaluation.

## Common mistakes

- **AI pages showing only cherry-picked outputs.** Teams optimize for immediate visual impact and hide the work
  needed to obtain it. Show selection and editing so visitors can judge effort as well as quality.
- **No data or rights information for AI tools.**
- **Physical products without delivery dates, returns or accurate colors.**
- **Portfolios with images but no reasoning.** Finished assets conceal who made the decisions. Explain role,
  constraints and one consequential trade-off without turning every card into a long essay.
- **Experimental sites with no way to the work or contact.**

## Checklist

- [ ] AI: real outputs with inputs, typical results, limits, data use, rights and price per use.
- [ ] Physical: detail, use, size, price, delivery date, returns and warranty near the action.
- [ ] Physical: variant imagery, dimensions and any material/color uncertainty support an informed choice.
- [ ] Studio: work first, project facts, process and decisions in cases.
- [ ] Studio: an accessible path to projects and contact even on experimental sites.
- [ ] Demonstrations distinguish selected highlights, ordinary results and unavailable capabilities.
- [ ] Loading, failure, changed conditions and uncertain receipt have truthful recovery paths.
- [ ] Success measures include result quality, purchase suitability or inquiry fit, with relevant guardrails.
