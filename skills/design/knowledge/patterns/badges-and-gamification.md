# Badges, achievements and gamification

Badges, levels, streaks and leaderboards can make progress visible and motivating — or can reward empty activity,
create anxiety and pressure people to use a product more than is good for them. This pattern covers when gamification
helps, how to reward real value, competition and consent, and how to avoid manipulation.

See also [money and progress](money-and-progress.md) for goals and streaks, and
[ethical persuasion](../12-psychology-and-ethical-persuasion.md).

## The problem and who has it

- **People learning or building a habit** (language learning, fitness, savings) who benefit from visible progress.
- **Communities** where recognition matters (contributors, sellers, reviewers).
- **Products under pressure to increase engagement**, which is where gamification is most often misused.

## Questions to ask before deciding

- **What real value does the person achieve**, and can badges reflect it?
- **Is the motivation intrinsic** (the person cares about the goal) or would rewards replace it?
- **Who sees achievements**, and do people consent to being compared?
- **Could gamification push harmful behavior** (overtraining, spending, staying up to keep a streak)?
- **Is the audience vulnerable** (children, health, money)?

## When to use it and when not

| Good fit | Poor fit |
| --- | --- |
| Learning milestones that represent real skill | Badges for logging in |
| Fitness personal records | Daily streaks for health that punish rest |
| Recognition of community contributions | Rewards for volume over quality (reviews written, posts made) |
| Progress in a long, structured program | Work tools where badges trivialize the work |

## Rewarding real value

- **Tie achievements to outcomes**, not activity: "Finished your first marathon training plan" rather than "Opened the
  app 30 days in a row".
- **Avoid rewarding volume** where quality matters: a badge for "100 reviews" encourages low-quality reviews.
- **Celebrate rarely enough** that it means something.

## Anatomy and behavior

- **Earned and available separated** (tabs Earned · Available · Leaderboard;
  [Garmin Connect](https://mobbin.com/screens/32a02223-ee10-4f46-b417-c4b38ac51889)); unearned ones grayed with the name
  visible — and a text equivalent, not only gray.
- **Progress toward each** ("8/100 watched", "1/5 weeks"; [Grab](https://mobbin.com/screens/16e7f3b8-3542-46de-b088-0dc70043bba8))
  and the date on earned ones ([Quizlet](https://mobbin.com/screens/2e13ecb7-2dd9-4116-b7bb-e7ba3f306680),
  [Me+](https://mobbin.com/screens/c4485d6d-f960-41f8-89df-4bb4d8f668c5)).
- **Level with a bar to the next** ("18 points to level 3").
- **Personal records** as their own group, with value and date
  ([Nike Run Club](https://mobbin.com/screens/eae85157-09eb-44ed-80d1-4165e9562663)).
- **Streaks with protection** ("2 streak freezes ready to use"; [LinkedIn](https://mobbin.com/screens/9a775693-d284-4ca6-a590-8fa36db0d8da))
  so a missed day is not punished.
- **Leaderboards among people you know and with consent** ("You only see contacts who turned this on"; LinkedIn), and
  your own position always visible.
- **Limited and seasonal editions** separate from permanent ones
  ([Ladder](https://mobbin.com/screens/e46f9534-8bcf-4383-8610-ae8a4d09d684)) — without manufactured urgency.
- **"More coming"** when the catalog grows, instead of an abrupt end.

## Streaks and habit loops in depth

Streaks are the most copied mechanic from habit apps (see [product archetypes](../39-product-archetypes.md)). They
work by making absence costly, which is exactly why they need care.

### What streaks do well, and what they cost

- **They lower the cost of showing up** when the unit is tiny. Duolingo's product team has described moving the
  streak from an XP goal to one lesson a day as one of its biggest engagement gains (as reported in interviews;
  figures unverified). The lesson: the size of the daily unit matters more than the reward.
- **They measure presence, not progress.** Critics of streak-heavy apps note high-frequency, low-depth use: the
  streak can do more motivational work than the learning itself. Evidence that streaks create lasting habits, as
  opposed to engagement, is thin.
- **They create a loss to avoid**, and the relief from that loss can be sold (freezes and repairs for gems). Selling
  relief from anxiety the product created is a business decision; do not import it as a pattern.

### Choosing the cadence and the rules

| Situation | Option | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily practice is genuinely useful (language drills, medication) | Daily streak with freezes earned by activity and rest days | Presence matters; forgiveness prevents collapse |
| Progress needs depth, not frequency (budgeting, training plans, writing) | Weekly goal ("3 sessions this week") | Matches the real rhythm; a missed day costs nothing |
| Health, recovery, mental health | No streak; history and gentle reminders the person set | Pressure to show up can be harmful (training through injury, guilt in low moments) |
| Children | No loss-framed streaks; celebrate effort predictably | Children are more sensitive to pressure; parents expect restraint |
| Work tools | No streaks | Work rhythms are not daily habits; badges trivialize work |

### Forgiveness built in

- **Freezes earned, not only bought**: Brilliant's "Streak Charge" is earned by doing the activity
  ([Brilliant](https://mobbin.com/screens/d9f1ce10-de3a-481e-b2a0-bbac8ece55a6)); Noom applies a freeze automatically
  and explains it ([Noom](https://mobbin.com/screens/2d907eb5-3af1-4d55-965a-b3d37c43904e)).
- **Repair without shame**: Finch opens with "It's okay to miss a day. The important thing is you're here today" and
  offers a free first repair ([Finch](https://mobbin.com/screens/d6e7a7b1-4b61-40b1-8ff7-1c602badec85)).
- **Plans that adapt instead of streaks that break**: Runna asks whether to rearrange or skip missed workouts
  ([Runna](https://mobbin.com/screens/321454f7-be6f-48af-999a-4bb5f4d2f09d)) — the best model for goals with a plan.
- **Keep the best run** visible after a break, so history is not erased.
- **Proportionate loss state**: Cal AI's full-screen "1 Day streak lost" with a grey flame
  ([Cal AI](https://mobbin.com/screens/f48da1ad-4de3-4900-9abd-6978316b5bb7)) gives a one-day lapse the weight of a
  failure; a small line in the history would do.

### Anatomy of a respectful streak

- A calendar of practiced days with freezes marked, so the record is honest
  ([Duolingo streak](https://mobbin.com/flows/d1739059-58c7-4b20-8630-8833869d34bb)).
- The rule stated plainly ("Do one lesson a day to extend your streak").
- Reminders at a time the person chose, at most once a day, with an easy way to change or stop them.
- Social streaks (friends) opt-in, with nudges limited and never automatic.
- A setting to hide the streak entirely.

### Celebration scale

Celebrate in proportion to the achievement and the audience: a small inline mark for a daily goal, a moment for a
week or a personal record, a full-screen moment only for rare milestones. Duolingo's lesson-end celebrations
([lesson completion](https://mobbin.com/flows/c199f9a9-7a91-4795-8ba5-5a3c24847009)) fit a short, playful session;
the same intensity after saving a document in a work tool is noise. Always offer reduced motion and sound off.

## Competition, consent and exclusion

- **Opt in** to leaderboards and public achievements.
- **Compare with oneself** by default (personal bests); social comparison as an option.
- **Fair comparisons**: group by level, age or goal; a beginner should not see only elite performers.
- **Hide or soften** ranking for people who are struggling; avoid public "last place".

## Avoiding manipulation

- No variable reward schedules (random prizes) in money, health or children's products.
- No loss-framing of streaks ("Don't lose your 120-day streak!") as pressure to use the product.
- Let people turn gamification off entirely.
- No hearts, energy or lives that stop the core activity to sell refills: it interrupts people when they practice
  hardest and turns a learning mechanic into a paywall.
- No mascots that sulk, cry or guilt in notifications.
- Do not tie real-world value (money, discounts) to compulsive behavior.

## Accessibility specifics

- Each badge has a readable name and description for screen readers.
- Progress bars with text values.
- Celebrations respect reduced motion; no flashing effects.

## Copy

- Badge names that describe the achievement in the person's language, not puns that do not translate.
- Encouraging, not guilt-inducing, messages.

## Common mistakes and why they happen

- **Badges for activity** — because engagement metrics drive the roadmap.
- **Daily streaks for everything.**
- **Public leaderboards by default.**
- **No off switch.**

## Worked comparison

**Weak.** A budgeting app awards "Big Spender" badges for transactions and shows a daily streak for opening the app.
People open it to keep the streak without budgeting.

**Strong.** The app recognizes "First month under budget", "Emergency fund: 3 months saved", with progress toward each;
streaks are weekly ("budget reviewed this week") with freezes; leaderboards do not exist; celebration is calm.

## How to verify and measure

- **Measure** whether gamified features correlate with real outcomes (skills learned, money saved), not only sessions.
- **Watch** for harmful patterns: late-night sessions to keep streaks, low-quality volume.
- **Ask** people whether achievements feel meaningful or pressuring.
