# Safety, reporting and moderation

Any product where people interact — messaging, comments, profiles, marketplaces, communities, dating, reviews — will
see harassment, spam, scams and abuse. Safety design gives people control over who can reach them, makes reporting
easy and meaningful, supports moderators, and treats the people who report and the people reported fairly. It also
protects people who are most at risk: those targeted for their identity, minors, people in abusive relationships.

## The problem and who has it

- **People being harassed or scammed** who need to stop it quickly and feel safe.
- **Bystanders** who see harmful content and want to report it.
- **Moderators** reviewing reports, often under pressure, often exposed to harmful content.
- **People reported** who deserve to know why actions were taken and to appeal.

## Questions to ask before deciding

- **What kinds of interaction** does the product allow (messages, comments, profiles, meetings in person)?
- **Who can contact whom** by default?
- **What harms are most likely** in this product and audience?
- **Who reviews reports** (automated systems, staff, community moderators) and how fast?
- **What actions exist** (remove content, warn, restrict, suspend, ban) and are they proportionate?
- **What legal obligations apply** in each region (reporting illegal content, protecting minors, transparency)?

## Prevention: control over contact and visibility

- **Contact defaults** that protect: message requests from non-connections, comments limited to followers, profile
  visibility controls.
- **Blocking**: immediate, effective across the product (messages, comments, profile views where possible), without
  notifying the blocked person by default.
- **Muting and restricting**: softer options that hide content without the other person knowing.
- **Filters**: hide messages or comments with chosen words; hide content from new accounts.
- **Privacy of presence and location** (online status, read receipts, location sharing) under the person's control.
- **Safety prompts** at risky moments ("This link was flagged as suspicious", "Meeting someone in person? Here are
  safety tips").

## Reporting

### Anatomy and behavior

- **Report reachable where the harm is**: on the message, the comment, the profile, the listing — in its menu, not
  only in a help center.
- **Short, guided categories** ("Harassment", "Spam or scam", "Hate", "Nudity", "Self-harm", "Something else"), with
  sub-options only when they change handling.
- **Context captured automatically** (the content, surrounding messages, the account), with notice to the reporter.
- **Optional detail** in free text; never required.
- **Immediate options during reporting**: block the person, hide the content, leave the conversation.
- **Confirmation with what happens next**: "We'll review this within 24 hours. You'll get an update in Support
  requests." And honesty when review takes longer.
- **Follow-up**: tell reporters the outcome where possible ("We removed the comment"), or at least that it was
  reviewed.

### Urgent situations

- For threats of violence, self-harm or exploitation of minors, provide immediate resources (emergency numbers by
  country, crisis lines) and prioritized review.
- Do not make a person in danger fill a long form.

## Moderation tools

- **A queue** with severity, category, reporter count and context.
- **Context in one view**: the reported content, the conversation around it, the account's history, previous reports.
- **Actions proportionate to harm**: remove content, warn, limit features, suspend temporarily, ban; with templates for
  explanations.
- **Protecting moderators**: blur graphic media by default, grayscale options, limits on exposure, easy escalation.
- **Consistency**: guidelines visible in the tool; decisions recorded with reasons.
- **Appeals routed to a different reviewer** when possible.

## The person reported

- **Explain what happened**: which content, which rule, which action.
- **Proportionate consequences** and a path to restore access (time-limited restrictions).
- **Appeals**: a clear way to ask for review, with an expected time.
- **Avoid revealing the reporter's identity.**

## Vulnerable people

- **Minors**: stricter defaults for contact and visibility; no contact from unknown adults; age-appropriate reporting.
- **People targeted for identity**: filters, rapid response to coordinated harassment, the ability to lock down
  quickly ("Limit interactions for 7 days").
- **People in abusive relationships**: stalkerware-aware features (location sharing visible and easy to stop), discreet
  exits, the ability to see logged-in devices.

## Languages and cultures

- Reporting categories and safety resources localized, including emergency numbers per country.
- Moderation capacity in the languages people use; automated detection quality varies by language — do not assume
  equal performance.
- Cultural context matters for what is harmful; guidelines need local expertise.

## States and edge cases

- Reports on content that was edited or deleted after reporting.
- Mass false reports used to silence someone.
- Blocked people creating new accounts.
- Group conversations where one member is blocked.

## Accessibility specifics

- Report and block actions keyboard and screen reader accessible, with clear names.
- Safety resources readable and reachable quickly.
- Moderation tools usable by moderators with disabilities.

## Copy

- Calm, supportive language for people reporting harm; never blaming.
- Clear rules in plain language.
- Explanations of actions specific and respectful.

## Common mistakes and why they happen

- **Report buried in help pages.**
- **Required free-text fields** for people in distress.
- **No feedback to reporters** — they stop reporting.
- **Blocking that only hides messages** but still allows profile viewing or mentions.
- **No appeals.**
- **Moderators exposed to graphic content without protection.**

## Worked comparison: harassment in comments

**Weak.** A creator receives dozens of abusive comments. To report, she must open each comment's menu, choose from 15
categories, write a description, and wait without updates. Blocking hides comments only from her, not from her
audience.

**Strong.** She can select several comments and report them together, or turn on "Limit interactions for 7 days"
(only followers of 30 days can comment). Blocking removes the person's ability to comment, message or mention her.
She gets an update: "We removed 18 comments and restricted 4 accounts."

## How to verify and measure

- **Measure** time to review reports, repeat harassment after action, appeals and overturn rates, reporter satisfaction.
- **Test** reporting flows with people who have experienced harassment (with care and consent), and moderation tools
  with moderators.
