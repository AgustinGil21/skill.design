# Case: a landing page that brings visits but few trials

## Context and evidence status

**Fictional case.** Agendo, the clinics, research observations, plan terms, quantities and example copy here are
invented for teaching. They are not customer evidence, approved claims or measured conversion results.
The proposed tests have not been performed; any real implementation needs verified product and service facts.

Agendo provides scheduling, online booking, text reminders, patient records and invoicing for small clinics.
The fictional market is physiotherapy practices in Spain and Portugal, with Spanish and Portuguese content.
Owners often also treat patients, so evaluating software competes with limited administrative time.
The person using the product daily may include reception staff who do not make the purchase decision.

The commercial goal is starting suitable trials. The visitor's goal is deciding whether the product can reduce
administrative work without disrupting appointments or creating a difficult migration.
A visually impressive page that obscures setup effort may increase clicks while attracting unsuitable trials.

Tone should be practical and credible, with enough warmth for a small-clinic audience.
The healthcare category does not automatically require institutional language or a sterile visual style.
The existing brand can remain recognizable while claims and service limits become more precise.

## Reframing the request

The fictional request is: "Conversion is poor. Make the page look more modern."
"Modern" describes an aesthetic preference, not a demonstrated cause of abandonment.
The design question is: **Does the page answer the arriving visitor's problem with credible evidence and a fitting
next step?**

Investigate three possible gaps before choosing visual treatment:

- Message match: the advertisement promises help with missed appointments, but the page talks broadly about management.
- Confidence: migration effort, price and patient participation remain unanswered.
- Continuity: the trial asks for effort or data the landing page did not prepare the owner to provide.

Visual craft still matters. Legibility, hierarchy, image quality and brand recognition can affect understanding.
The reasoning is to separate these contributions so a complete redesign does not conceal which problem was solved.

## Evidence and its limits

| Fictional evidence | Inference | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search concerns no-shows and booking | Test intent-specific messaging | Queries omit some objections |
| Many visits are on phones | Mobile reading and forms matter | Device does not identify the decision-maker |
| Hero promises "The future of clinic management" | Vague immediate promise | Copy alone cannot explain outcomes |
| Sales notes mention import and cost | Answer these objections | Callers differ from silent visitors |
| Owners ask whether patients will book online | Explain supported patient journeys | Do not stereotype by age alone |
| Activation is not linked to source | Reporting is incomplete | Analytics do not replace interviews |

For a real project, inspect the exact advertisement, query category, landing version and next-step flow together.
A campaign average can hide a good booking page and a poorly matched no-show page.
Check whether consent, blockers or cross-device visits create missing analytics before interpreting drop-off.

Read sales notes for the question behind an objection.
"Will older patients use it?" may mean digital confidence, accessibility, language, device access or preference.
The answer should describe supported alternatives and evidence rather than treating age as a capability measure.

The scenario assumes permission could be obtained for named clinic stories and product screenshots.
That is a production dependency, not permission to fabricate a testimonial or publish patient data.
If proof is unavailable, show a truthful demonstration of the workflow and state what remains unverified.

## Questions and constraints before deciding

- Which search promises send qualified clinic owners, and which attract patients looking for appointments?
- Is the primary next step self-serve trial, assisted setup or a sales conversation?
- What does the trial include, when does it end and what happens to imported information afterward?
- Is import free for every supported system, or only some file types and account sizes?
- Can a clinic keep phone booking while using reminders, and what does the staff workflow look like?
- Does sending a text reminder have an additional cost or destination limitation?
- Which claims have dated source evidence and explicit permission for publication?
- What facts differ between the Spanish and Portuguese offers beyond language?

The fictional team has one designer and one developer for four weeks.
That constraint favors a focused content and structure release within the existing visual system.
It does not justify publishing unverified hosting, security, migration or savings claims to fill the page.

Health-data obligations need qualified review in the actual market.
EU hosting alone is not evidence that the entire service or customer workflow meets every relevant obligation.
The landing page should point to verified controls and current service terms instead of using geography as a badge
that implies a complete compliance conclusion.

## Alternatives and the recommendation

| Situation | Option | Why consider it | Cost or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Content works, visuals fail | Visual refresh | Improves hierarchy | Does not address message mismatch |
| One dominant intent | Rewrite one focused page | Clear story, low upkeep | May underserve other audiences |
| Distinct search intentions | Focused pages per intent | Continues arrival promise | More content upkeep |
| Brand and positioning both fail | Full redesign | Coordinates change | Harder to isolate causes |
| Promise is unsupported | Repair offer or onboarding | Prevents misleading acquisition | Delays release |

**Recommendation: focused no-show and booking pages, with a broader main page using the same system.**
Start with the main intent mismatch, then test whether a visual refresh adds value.
This accepts a less dramatic first release and the ongoing cost of keeping shared facts synchronized.

The visual-only option is rejected because the fictional evidence points to unanswered task questions.
A full rebrand is deferred because it introduces many variables and exceeds the immediate learning need.
A separate page for every small keyword variation is also rejected: it adds maintenance without a distinct argument.
Group pages by materially different visitor questions rather than by superficial phrase changes.

If traffic is too sparse to maintain and evaluate several pages, use one strong core page with source-appropriate
entry sections and stable shared claims. The recommended structure is conditional on useful intent differences.
If the trial cannot deliver the import promise, the offer must change before acquisition copy does.

## The proposed argument, section by section

### Hero: continue the arrival promise

For the fictional no-show page, the headline connects booking and reminders to the visitor's problem.
An illustrative English version is: "Online booking and text reminders for your clinic."
A supporting sentence explains that staff can keep taking appointments by phone where the product supports it.
Avoid promising fewer no-shows as a guaranteed outcome unless the wording and supporting evidence justify that claim.

The primary action names the next step, such as a trial, and the adjacent text states verified terms.
The original scenario uses a 14-day trial with no card and free import; these are fictional offer settings.
A real page should not inherit them simply because the example sounds persuasive.
The screenshot shows the actual booking or reminder workflow using clearly fictional patient information.

### Proof: answer the relevant doubt

Place a permitted, relevant clinic story near the claim it supports.
A reminder workflow demonstration proves the mechanism exists; it does not prove a particular reduction in missed
visits.
A clinic outcome needs context: period, denominator, other process changes and the limits of attribution.
A named person and photograph require permission; neither is a substitute for substantiating the result.

This case intentionally supplies no invented performance figure to paste into production.
The content slot is a requirement for verified evidence, not an invitation to create a plausible statistic.
If proof is still being collected, use an honest product example and a narrower claim.

### Objections: make evaluation possible

The migration section explains supported sources, required input, responsibility and expected process.
"Usually ready in a day" needs actual service evidence and a defined start point; otherwise describe the steps.
Keep sensitive import work behind an authenticated process rather than asking for patient records on a marketing form.

Pricing explains the charging unit, relevant message costs, currency and tax treatment for the offer.
A fictional small-clinic calculation can teach the model only if visibly marked as an example.
The calculator or example should not conceal minimum charges, setup fees or recurring commitments.

Patient-adoption content describes booking channels and reminder behavior without claiming every patient will
participate.
Trust content links the question to verified service facts, such as documented access controls or data-processing terms.
A generic lock illustration adds little if the actual responsibility and control remain unexplained.

### Closing and next step

Repeat the primary action after the main objections have been answered.
An assisted conversation can be a secondary path when self-serve evaluation is unsuitable.
Explain what that contact entails so an owner does not mistake a sales request for immediate product access.
After sign-up, orientation and import guidance should deliver the continuity promised by the page.

## States and edge cases

| State | Visitor information | Next action or transition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First visit | Relevant offer, terms and evidence | Evaluate or start trial |
| Media unavailable | Core promise still readable in text | Continue without screenshot or video |
| Pricing unavailable | No fabricated fallback amount | View verified terms or contact the service |
| Form empty | Required information and why it is needed | Complete minimal fields |
| Validation error | Specific field problem; entered data retained | Correct and retry |
| Submitting | Request pending, not yet a created account | Wait without repeated submission |
| Existing account | Safe route to account access | Sign in or recover access as appropriate |
| Request timeout | Account outcome uncertain | Check confirmation before resubmitting |
| Offline | Submission unavailable; non-sensitive input retained if safe | Reconnect and retry |
| Trial created | Confirmed next step and offer terms | Configure clinic or review import guidance |
| Verification pending | Explain email verification and return path | Open email or resend under stated rules |
| Unsupported import | Limits and alternative setup path | Use supported format or request help |
| Different market | Local offer and available service | Deliberately choose language or region |

An existing-account response needs review against the service's account-discovery policy.
Helpful recovery should not unnecessarily disclose whether an arbitrary email address has an account.
A generic response may be appropriate where disclosure is sensitive; usability testing must cover that wording too.

A returning visitor may have read on a phone and now evaluate on desktop.
Preserve the same offer terms across devices; responsive layout is not a reason to present a different commitment.
Regional routing should allow a deliberate override rather than forcing language from an IP address.

## Copy by moment and localization

| Moment | Function | Fictional English illustration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Name the service and relevant task | "Online booking and text reminders for your clinic" |
| Trial action | Identify the next commitment | "Start the trial" |
| Trial terms | Clarify duration and payment conditions | "14 days. No card required." |
| Migration | Explain scope rather than vague ease | "See which appointment files we can import" |
| Alternative channel | Address patient choice | "Keep taking bookings by phone" |
| Error | Help correct the input | "Enter an email address where you can receive the setup message." |
| Pending | Avoid false completion | "Creating your clinic account" |
| Success | Connect acquisition to first value | "Your account is ready. Review your clinic setup." |

These are English illustrations of functions; the intended Spanish and Portuguese versions need native-market review.
Do not impose English headline fragments, capitalization or verb-first actions when natural local syntax differs.
Form of address should fit clinic-owner expectations and remain consistent through onboarding and support.

Treat language and market as separate dimensions. A Portuguese-speaking owner may operate outside Portugal;
the offer's availability, billing currency and service limits should be explicit rather than inferred from language
alone.
Review local appointment terminology, tax display, phone-number conventions and preferred contact methods.
Names should follow how the person writes them rather than forcing an English first-name/last-name model.

Plan for expansion in hero text, field labels and action buttons; shorter translations need balanced layout too.
Keep dates and durations unambiguous, and use plural-aware messages for practitioners or reminders.
If the product expands to right-to-left scripts, test navigation, mixed-direction contact details and screenshot
context.
A screenshot in another language may obscure the mechanism even when the surrounding page is translated.

The repository's copy detector is an aid only for languages it actually supports.
Do not claim that passing `slop.mjs` validates Portuguese copy without verifying that a Portuguese ruleset exists.
Native editorial review evaluates meaning and suitability beyond any word-list scan.

## Accessibility and performance

Relevant WCAG 2.2 requirements for the implemented page include:

- **1.1.1 Non-text Content, A:** informative screenshots need an appropriate text alternative.
- **1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), AA:** text meets the applicable contrast thresholds and exceptions.
- **1.4.10 Reflow, AA:** content works at the specified narrow equivalent width, subject to its exceptions.
- **2.1.1 Keyboard, A:** navigation, trial form and consent choices work without a pointer.
- **2.4.7 Focus Visible, AA:** the current keyboard target remains visible.
- **3.3.1 Error Identification, A; 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions, A:** form errors and required inputs are
  understandable.
- **3.1.1 Language of Page, A:** each language version declares the appropriate page language.

Recommendations include keeping essential hero copy as real text and placing labels outside fields.
A screenshot's alternative should communicate relevant information, not transcribe an unreadable entire application.
Nearby prose can explain the workflow when a dense image cannot serve as its accessible equivalent alone.

Choose image format and dimensions from actual assets and supported delivery, not a rule that every hero must use AVIF.
Preload only a justified critical resource; excess preload can compete with fonts or other content.
Measure loading and interaction on representative phones and networks before claiming performance success.
A static contrast scan or a design review cannot establish a keyboard, screen reader or performance pass.

## Worked comparison: the same incoming clinic owner

**Weak version.** An owner clicks an advertisement about missed appointments.
The hero says "The future of clinic management" above a decorative dashboard.
A generic quote claims transformation, while import details and message costs are absent.
The trial form asks for extensive clinic information and then requires an unexpected setup call.
The page may look polished, but it postpones the decisions the visitor came to make.

**Stronger version.** The hero explains booking and reminders, with a real workflow demonstration.
Verified terms clarify the trial; migration content states supported inputs and responsibilities.
Relevant proof is contextualized rather than universalized, and patient booking alternatives remain visible.
The form requests only information needed for the next step, which matches the promised evaluation path.

The stronger version may be longer and less visually dramatic.
Its advantage is a coherent argument from search intent through first use, not the assumption that longer pages convert.
If qualified owners primarily need assisted procurement, a clear consultation path may outperform self-serve trials.

## Proposed test protocol

Recruit clinic decision-makers in each market and include staff who will operate the workflow.
Use fictional clinic and patient data. Avoid asking participants to enter real health information in a prototype.
Evaluate the existing and proposed page against the same tasks, with order controlled where comparison is used.

1. Show the advertisement and arrival page; ask what service and next step the participant expects.
2. After brief exposure, ask what they recall without treating recall as proof of purchase confidence.
3. Allow normal reading and ask how migration, pricing and patient booking would work for their clinic.
4. Ask them to decide whether the offer fits; a well-informed refusal can be a successful comprehension outcome.
5. Complete sign-up, an error recovery and the first setup step to test promise continuity.
6. Repeat critical reading and form tasks with mobile, zoom, keyboard and assistive technology.

Record misconceptions, unanswered objections, assistance and incorrect expectations about commitment.
Do not ask only which page looks more modern; preference cannot explain whether the offer was understood.
Native reviewers assess claims and terminology in context, including error messages and emails after sign-up.
A working implementation is needed to verify real performance and account-state behavior.

## Measurement and guardrails

Primary acquisition measure: suitable trials created among eligible visitors, with a defined event and denominator.
Quality measure: trials reaching a meaningful first-value event, such as configuring and sending an eligible reminder.
Do not send real reminders simply to improve activation; the workflow requires appropriate customer authorization.
Use aggregated product events without recording patient content in marketing analytics.

Track acquisition source, market and device where consent and measurement quality permit.
Keep visitor assignment consistent if running a controlled experiment, and define the analysis before inspecting
results.
A four-week duration alone cannot establish adequate power or account for campaign changes.
When traffic is modest, qualitative testing and cautious cohort observation may be more useful than a noisy winner
label.

Guardrails include unqualified sign-ups, setup abandonment, support burden, unwanted contact, refunds and trust
complaints.
More clicks on migration content can mean interest or confusion; inspect the subsequent task and interview evidence.
A trial increase without activation may indicate a more persuasive page that sets the wrong expectation.
No numerical conversion improvement is asserted by this fictional case.

Reconsider the recommendation if migration cannot meet the offer, local pricing differs materially,
patient-facing traffic dominates the campaign, or buyers need procurement evidence rather than a trial.
A page cannot compensate for an unsuitable product, inaccessible booking flow or unsupported service promise.
The visual refresh should be judged by the same comprehension and quality measures when it eventually occurs.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Arrival intent, decision-maker and next commitment are explicit.
- [ ] Claims and clinic proof have real sources, context, ownership and publication permission.
- [ ] Migration, message costs and trial terms match the delivered service.
- [ ] Language and market differences extend beyond translation.
- [ ] Form, uncertain submission and first-use states preserve the page's promise.
- [ ] Accessibility and performance claims are tied to actual verification.
- [ ] Comparison testing evaluates understanding and informed choice, not visual preference alone.
- [ ] Metrics include suitable activation and guardrails against misleading acquisition.
