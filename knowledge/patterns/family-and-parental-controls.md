# Family accounts and parental controls

Family features serve two people at once: an adult who wants to protect and manage, and a child or teenager who uses
the product and deserves a good, age-appropriate experience and growing autonomy. Designing only for the adult creates
surveillance tools children work around; designing only for the child ignores real risks. This pattern covers profiles,
age-based restrictions, purchase controls, autonomy by stage, shared devices and recovery.

## The problem and who has it

- **Parents and guardians** managing what children can see, buy and share.
- **Children and teenagers** using the product within limits that should make sense to them.
- **Families sharing devices and subscriptions.**

When these features fail: children see content they should not, make unintended purchases, or are locked into
childish experiences as they grow; parents cannot find or understand the controls; teenagers lose trust because of
hidden monitoring.

## Questions to ask before deciding

- **Which ages** does the product serve, and do legal requirements apply (child privacy laws vary by country)?
- **What are the risks**: inappropriate content, purchases, contact with strangers, sharing personal data?
- **What autonomy is appropriate** at each age, and how does it grow?
- **Is the device shared or personal?**
- **What does the child see** about the controls applied to them?
- **How is the adult's control protected** (PIN, biometrics) and recovered?

## Alternatives and how to choose

| Situation | Option |
| --- | --- |
| Young children on a shared device | Separate child profile with curated content, protected exit |
| Older children with own devices | Supervised accounts with age-based defaults, linked to a parent |
| Teenagers | Teen accounts with more autonomy, transparent supervision, gradually reduced controls |
| Purchases | Approval requests ("Ask to buy") rather than total blocks |
| Shared streaming at home | Profiles per person with ratings per profile |

## Autonomy and protection by stage

| Stage | Protection | Autonomy | Transparency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Young child | Curated content, no purchases, no contact | Choose within a safe set | Simple explanations ("This is your space") |
| Pre-teen | Ratings, purchase approvals, limited contact | Choose content within ratings, request access | The child knows what is restricted and why |
| Teen | Fewer restrictions, safety defaults | Manage own settings with some locked by guardian | Supervision is visible to the teen |

## Anatomy and behavior

- **A family center** with children's profiles, and "Add a child profile" and "Invite a teen" as distinct paths
  ([YouTube](https://mobbin.com/screens/5aba22e7-99cc-4e6b-9532-fda528a4ca1a),
  [Amazon Alexa](https://mobbin.com/screens/01d88b08-9323-4446-bb1c-5b1fff60bbdf)).
- **Age restrictions as single choices with examples** of what each level includes (for example "Ages 7–9", with the
  rating labels used in that country) and "How ratings work"
  ([Tubi](https://mobbin.com/screens/6a39e7df-350a-4ed7-bcd0-4e939c865952),
  [Plex](https://mobbin.com/screens/506d4299-8d73-45f0-bc1b-f98ac9b45f9e)). Rating systems differ by country; show the
  local one.
- **Kids mode as a switch with what it turns on** ("Includes parental controls and more privacy") and an exit protected
  with a PIN ([HBO Max](https://mobbin.com/screens/ffb49833-1f15-408d-abcd-ac664b2daf42),
  [Paramount+](https://mobbin.com/screens/00db3918-13b4-4b48-8779-58f4f671acb5)).
- **Separate content restrictions from purchase restrictions**, each with its PIN or biometrics
  ([Prime Video](https://mobbin.com/screens/cdbdbd94-480c-4d76-a09c-bd89cb2b0d3e)).
- **View and manage from the adult side**: history, blocked content, shared lists
  ([Spotify Kids](https://mobbin.com/screens/015189f5-71f1-4eb9-a6b4-baee2db18503)).
- **Say what is not covered** ("Advanced restrictions can only be set on the web", Plex) and the default ("May not be
  suitable for all children").
- **The children's privacy policy linked in place**; collect no more data from the child than necessary.

### Requests instead of walls

When a child hits a restriction, offer a request: "Ask a parent to unlock this". The parent receives the request with
context and can approve once, always, or decline with an explanation the child sees.

## Shared devices and recovery

- **Profile switching** protected for adult profiles; quick for children's profiles.
- **Forgot the PIN**: recover through the adult account with authentication, never through the child profile.
- **When a child ages up**: suggest graduating to a teen or adult account, with the child's involvement.
- **When a family separates or a guardian changes**: support transferring management.

## States and edge cases

- Content without a rating: default to restricted for young profiles, and say so.
- Live content and user-generated content that cannot be pre-rated.
- Purchases made through app stores, governed by store-level family settings.
- Children in different countries than the parent's account.

## Accessibility specifics

- PIN entry accessible, with alternatives for adults with motor disabilities (biometrics, longer timeouts).
- Child profiles readable and operable for children with disabilities (large targets, simple language, audio).
- Restrictions explained in text, not only icons.

## Copy

- To parents: plain explanations of what each setting does and does not do.
- To children: friendly, honest, not patronizing; explain restrictions ("This needs a parent's OK").
- Ratings named as in the local system.

## Common mistakes and why they happen

- **Hidden monitoring of teenagers** — erodes trust and drives workarounds.
- **One age level for all children.**
- **Locks without request paths.**
- **Recovery through the child's profile.**
- **Collecting unnecessary data from children.**

## Worked comparison

**Weak.** A single "Kids mode" toggle that hides everything but cartoons, with an unprotected exit; purchases blocked
entirely; a 12-year-old finds it embarrassing and uses the adult profile instead.

**Strong.** Profiles by age level with local ratings; purchases as "Ask to buy" requests; the 12-year-old sees which
content is restricted and can request access; the parent approves from their phone; the adult profile is protected
with a PIN.

## How to verify and measure

- **Test** with parents and with children of different ages (with consent and care).
- **Measure** use of request flows, PIN resets, workarounds (children on adult profiles), complaints about
  inappropriate content.
