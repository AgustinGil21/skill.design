# Recipes: complete landing pages section by section

Skeletons to start from — not templates to fill. Text in brackets is replaced with facts about the product; if a
bracket cannot be filled with something real, the section is removed. Each recipe states when it fits, the reasoning
behind the order, and what to cut first.

Before using a recipe, do the [strategy](01-strategy-and-structure.md): goal, audience, stage, objections and proof.
A recipe chosen without them produces the generic page this skill tries to avoid.

## How to use a recipe

1. Pick the recipe closest to the page type and the visitor's stage.
2. Map each section to a visitor question and to proof you actually have.
3. Remove sections without proof; merge sections that answer the same question.
4. Reorder for the visitor's stage (comparison earlier for switchers, pricing earlier for ready-to-buy visitors).
5. Write all the copy in a document before designing (see [persuasive writing](04-persuasive-writing.md)).

## Software for teams (SaaS)

**Fits:** self-serve or hybrid software, problem-aware visitors, a product that can be shown.

1. **Navigation**: logo · Product · Pricing · Customers · Resources · Sign in · [Try free]
2. **Hero**: "[Outcome] for [audience]" · "[Product] [does X] so that [benefit]." · [Try free] [Watch a 2-min demo]
   · "Free up to [limit]. No card." · a large real screenshot of the main workflow.
3. **Logos**: "Used by [N] teams in [industries]" + 6–8 real logos, with permission.
4. **Before / now**: four parallel rows of today's pain and how it changes.
5. **How it works**: three steps with a screenshot each.
6. **Features**: a grid with one large card (the key feature) + four medium ones, each with a real fragment.
7. **Integrations**: "Works with what you already use" + logos by category + a link to the directory.
8. **Proof**: two or three cases with a figure ("[Customer] reduced [metric] by [N] %"), a person with photo and role.
9. **Security**: certifications with their meaning + where data lives.
10. **Pricing summary**: three plans + "See the full comparison".
11. **FAQ**: six to eight real objections.
12. **Closing**: "[The hero's idea said differently]" + button + risk reducers.
13. **Footer**: site map, legal, status, language, social.

**Why this order:** visitors first confirm what it is (hero), that others trust it (logos), that it addresses their
pain (before/now), that it is not hard (how it works), and what it does (features); objections follow once interest
exists.

**Cut first:** before/now if the hero already names the pain; security for low-risk products; integrations if there
are few.

**Variants:** sales-led → replace "Try free" with "Book a demo", move security and case studies up. Developer tools →
add a code sample or playground after the hero, documentation link in the navigation.

## AI tool (generation)

**Fits:** tools whose output can be shown; visitors skeptical about quality, control and data.

1. **Hero**: a headline about what is created ("Product videos ready for social in minutes") + a prompt field or a
   live example + a gallery of real results below.
2. **Gallery by use case**, with each result's prompt, copyable.
3. **Input → output**: one example step by step (what you upload, what you get, how long it takes).
4. **Control**: what can be adjusted (style, brand, length, later editing) with screenshots.
5. **For whom**: three audiences with the piece each one makes.
6. **Trust**: usage rights, privacy (no training on your data, if true), honest limits.
7. **Pricing** by credits or generations with a simple calculator.
8. **Social proof** from creators with their work.
9. **Closing**: "Try it with your first [piece]" + button.

**Why this order:** quality is the first doubt, so outputs come first; control and trust follow because they are the
next objections. See [AI, physical products and portfolios](12-ai-physical-products-and-portfolios.md).

## Physical product

**Fits:** a single hero product or a small range; direct-to-consumer.

1. **Hero**: photo or 3D of the object at its best angle + name + one line + price and [Buy].
2. **In use**: photo or video of the object in its real context, with three benefits.
3. **Detail**: materials, dimensions and weight annotated over the image.
4. **How it is used**: three steps or a short video.
5. **Comparison** with the alternative it replaces (no brand names unless necessary and fair).
6. **Reviews** with customer photos and a score with the number of reviews.
7. **Shipping, returns, warranty** in three columns.
8. **FAQ** + **closing** with the buy button and the price.

**Why this order:** desire first (object, use), then detail for rational confirmation, then risk reduction close to the
purchase.

## Waitlist

**Fits:** pre-launch products; the goal is qualified sign-ups and learning.

1. **Hero**: what it is (one sentence), when (month or season), [email] [Reserve my spot], "[N] people signed up" (if
   real).
2. **What you will be able to do**: three concrete things with an image or preview.
3. **Why sign up now**: early access, founder pricing (if real).
4. **Who is making it**: the team or the story in a paragraph.
5. **Closing** with the same field + a minimal footer.

See [mobile, launches and integrations](10-mobile-launches-and-integrations.md) for states after sign-up.

## Studio or portfolio

**Fits:** agencies, studios, freelancers; visitors judging quality and fit.

1. **Hero**: a positioning sentence ("We design digital products for [kind of clients]") + the best piece in motion.
2. **Selected work**: six to eight projects with image, client, discipline and result.
3. **Services** in a short list with what each includes.
4. **Process**: three or four stages.
5. **Clients and recognition**.
6. **Contact**: copyable email + "Book a call" + the kinds of projects they take.

## Comparison page ("[Product] vs. [Competitor]" or "[Competitor] alternative")

**Fits:** visitors actively comparing; search traffic for competitor names.

1. **Hero**: "The [competitor] alternative for [audience]" + the main difference in one line + [Try free].
2. **Summary table**: five to eight verifiable differences, with the date of the comparison.
3. **Why teams switch**: three reasons with customer quotes from people who switched.
4. **Where [competitor] may be better**: honest; it builds trust.
5. **Migration**: how to move, what is imported, how long it takes.
6. **Pricing comparison** for equivalent plans.
7. **Closing**.

**Rules:** facts only, sourced and dated; no mockery; update when the competitor changes; follow local law on
comparative advertising.

## Mobile app

**Fits:** consumer apps; most traffic on phones.

1. **Hero**: what the app does + the app in a phone in real use + store buttons (by platform) or QR on desktop.
2. **Three moments of use** with screenshots.
3. **Ratings and reviews** from the stores (real, current).
4. **Privacy**: what data is collected (consistent with store labels).
5. **Closing** with store buttons.

## Event or webinar

**Fits:** a dated event with registration.

1. **Hero**: title, date and time with time zone (and "add to calendar"), format (online, place), [Register].
2. **What you will learn**: three concrete outcomes.
3. **Speakers** with photos, roles and one line each.
4. **Agenda** with times.
5. **Registration form** (few fields) + what happens after (link, reminder, recording).
6. **After the event**: the page changes to the recording, not "Registration closed".

## Before writing code

- Write all the copy in a document and read it straight through: is it understood without images?
- Choose the visual idea (see [art direction and creativity](05-art-direction-and-creativity.md)).
- Mark the two to four motion moments (see [motion](06-motion.md)).
- Collect real proof (screenshots, figures, permissions for logos and testimonials).
- Decide the performance budget and the reduced-motion behavior.
- Test the skeleton with the longest language and on a phone.

## Common mistakes

- **Using a recipe without strategy.**
- **Keeping sections without proof** because the recipe has them.
- **Filling brackets with invented content.**
- **Never reordering for the visitor's stage.**
